Cross-border invoicing

How to invoice international clients in USD

If you and your client agreed to bill in US dollars, state USD on the invoice and give instructions for a payment method that can receive it. A bare “$” can mean something different to a client in Canada, Australia, or Singapore.

Create a USD invoice

Set up your USD invoice

  1. Confirm the amount, currency, payment method, and who covers transfer fees. If the quote was in another currency, agree on the USD amount before sending the invoice.
  2. In BillKit, select USD under Invoice details, then enter your USD rates and charges. Changing the currency changes how the amounts are displayed; it doesn’t convert the numbers.
  3. Use the client’s legal billing details, a unique invoice number and a specific due date. Add a purchase order or project reference if required.
  4. In Bank details / payment instructions, add the instructions your provider gives you for receiving USD. Ask the client to use the invoice number as the reference.
  5. Review applicable tax and invoice requirements, then choose Print / Save PDF and check the saved copy before sending it.

Example payment instructions

Invoice INV-0086 · Total due: USD 1,250.00 · Due: November 30, 2026

“Please remit USD 1,250.00 via the agreed bank transfer method. Use INV-0086 as the payment reference. Include the agreed transfer fees separately so the amount received is USD 1,250.00.”

Use the fee sentence only if that is what you agreed to. Include the relevant beneficiary, routing or SWIFT details supplied by your bank or provider. Use a secure channel to confirm new or changed payment instructions; never include a password, PIN or one-time code.

Currency, tax and settlement are separate

  • Invoice currency: What the client owes. If the preview shows a dollar symbol, add “All amounts in USD” in Notes so there’s no ambiguity in the PDF.
  • Settlement currency: What arrives in your account. Your bank or provider may convert the funds or deduct fees. Record what was actually received alongside the agreed invoice amount.
  • Tax treatment: Depends on where the parties are based, what you supply, and the rules that apply to you. Billing in USD does not by itself make a sale tax-free. Confirm required tax IDs, rates and wording before issuing the invoice.

BillKit creates the document, not the transfer. Its UPI QR feature applies to INR invoices, not USD. For line items and due dates, use the worked freelance invoice example.
